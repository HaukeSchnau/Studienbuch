# Planner first, with teacher flows behind a per-school capability

Status: accepted 2026-11-17 ([D93](https://files.schnau.dev/html/studienbuch-roadmap/#D93), [D95](https://files.schnau.dev/html/studienbuch-roadmap/#D95), [D96](https://files.schnau.dev/html/studienbuch-roadmap/#D96))

The base tier of Studienbuch is a personal planner. A student sees the timetable and substitutions and keeps their own homework. Grades, absences and a parent's signature are private notes in the student's notebook, sealed on their devices. Nothing a student writes reaches a teacher or the school.

The Lernplattform tier comes later in the same app. It covers homework a teacher assigns, grades or absences shared with the school, and parent confirmations the school receives. Each of these sits behind a capability of the school, a rule field on its `School` row that stays off unless the school's tier turns it on. Only the server writes a school's space, so no device can turn it on. The server checks it where it already decides who may write. It creates the spaces these flows need and grants the roles in them only in a school with the capability, so `Sync.mayWrite` and `Sync.maySeal` refuse every such write anywhere else. A readable mutator can also read the field, because the server runs it again.

Three rules hold for that tier. Official grade or attendance administration, if it comes, gets its own space kinds and tables apart from learning features, and never enters a notebook's content. Teachers get an online view with no local database on their own devices, an exception to ADR 0013, because Lower Saxony's MK decree of 1.1.2020, valid until 31.12.2027, limits what teachers keep on private devices. An absence that reaches the school carries one of fixed categories (krank, Arzttermin, familiär, sonstiges) plus an optional short note, which keeps readable health data (Art. 9 DSGVO) out of the school. The student's own notes keep free text.

No account, notebook or sync rule may assume that an account has a school, because consumer sales through in-app purchases may come later (D96). An account without a school already signs in and syncs. It has no notebook yet, because today a notebook exists only at a school.

LfD Niedersachsen's mandatory DSFA list, item 8, covers student data on learning platforms run by a central processor, and Studienbuch syncs through our server. A school that needs a DSFA makes us, as its processor, appoint a data-protection officer under § 38(1) sentence 2 BDSG, whatever our size. Encryption doesn't take a product off that list. The DSK's description of a learning platform centres on teachers providing content and organising learning with students, so a planner that holds only the student's own data has a defensible case to stay outside item 8, which the school records in its threshold analysis. The case is open. If a school's data-protection officer insists on a DSFA, we appoint ours before that school goes live, and in any case before the Lernplattform tier ships. A capability per school keeps the larger tier one product and one codebase, and the officer's cost arrives with the first school that buys it.
