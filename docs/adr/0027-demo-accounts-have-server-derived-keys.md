# Demo accounts have server-derived keys

Status: accepted 2026-10-29 ([D55](https://files.schnau.dev/html/studienbuch-roadmap/#D55), [D66](https://files.schnau.dev/html/studienbuch-roadmap/#D66))

Previews seed a demo school whose students, Lena and Tom, anyone can sign in as. On beta, the store's reviewers sign in to either account with a password from a secret. Their notebooks are encrypted like everyone else's, so a device needs the account's root key R to open them. For the accounts in `demo_account`, and no other, the server derives R from the secret `DEMO_KEY_SEED`: HKDF-SHA256 over the seed, with the environment and the account id in the info. Each environment keeps its own seed for as long as its database, and the environment in the info keeps beta's and production's keys apart even under one seed. The seed publishes the accounts' first key records. Persona sign-in on development and preview servers hands R over with the session. `Demo.rootKey` gives a signed-in demo account its own R on any server, which the review accounts need after signing in with their password.

This is the one exception to "the server holds no key", and demo accounts give up the privacy the others have. The server, and anyone who holds `DEMO_KEY_SEED`, can read everything a demo account seals, including what reviewers and testers type into its notebook. So can every holder of the review password, and on previews anyone on the Tailnet. Demo notebooks are for invented content only.

The exception stays small. Only `prepare`'s seed and the operator console create demo accounts, because they alone hold the `Seeding` capability. A lint rule keeps that capability, the derivation, persona sign-in and every write to `demo_account` in their own modules. A test calls every RPC as an ordinary account and finds no new demo account and no root key. A demo account never shares an encrypted space with a real account, because a key box sealed to it is readable by everyone above. Today `Access.admit` enforces this by letting demo accounts into the demo school only. Every later shared space, such as chats and family spaces, must refuse a demo member too.

What this leaves out: a demo account's R never changes, so demo accounts have no recovery methods and no renewal. Changing `DEMO_KEY_SEED` strands what their devices sealed. Demo content written on the server waits until devices open sealed rows. Until then a persona's first device opens the notebook's first key epoch with the R it received.

Reasoning: [encryption.md](https://files.schnau.dev/html/studienbuch-roadmap/doc.html?f=design/encryption.md), section 8, "Demo accounts", and [hosting.md](https://files.schnau.dev/html/studienbuch-roadmap/doc.html?f=design/hosting.md), "Seeding a demo school".
